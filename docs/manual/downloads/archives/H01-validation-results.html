H01 · Measured and predicted horizon altitudes

Working validation results · 8 November 2026 · skyscapeR 2.0.9004

How accurately does createHor() reproduce a surveyed terrain skyline?

Across 334 distinct bearings at 31 observing setups, the median absolute difference from the field measurement was 0.036°, the mean absolute difference was 0.169°, and 91.9% of predictions fell within ±0.5°. Mean signed error was +0.048° (predicted minus measured). Thus the DEM-derived skyline usually agreed with the observed terrain horizon to within half a degree at these bearings. Individual errors reached 7.26°. The 0.5° figure is an observed proportion for this sample and cannot serve as an uncertainty bound for an individual sightline.

Each residual combines the effects of the terrain model, observer position and height, numerical skyline calculation and field measurement. The surveys sample particular directions at each setup. Accuracy between those directions, including narrow peaks or notches, remains unmeasured. The station and terrain summaries below show how far the pooled result transfers across the tested conditions.

The longer-radius comparison includes 215 bearings from 29 survey setups. Each bearing contributes once. Positive residuals indicate that the computed horizon is higher. All errors are in degrees.

Coverage and interpretation

Ireland uses the 25 m EU-DEM to 65 km. Wales uses the native 1 m Welsh DTM to 50 km. Chaco uses USGS 1/3 arc-second terrain to 50 km. Tenerife uses the IGN 5 m DTM to 50 km with its published GPS positions and refraction-corrected field altitudes. The English controls use the 1 m DTM to 5 km. Malta and Orkney use an explicitly coarser SRTM comparison after gaps in the local lidar coverage. The archive separates these products and retains the incomplete native-resolution runs.

West Kennet reproduces the 12 previous predictions to numerical precision. At Rectory Farm the earlier exact-ray profiles trace several large differences to nearby terrain, approximately 245–289 m from the instrument. At Tal-Lippija the published −7.5° coastal sight produces the largest residual in this working sample. Its original label and measurement are retained.

What changed since the first run?

The earlier working comparison used 15 km in Wales and Tenerife and scored 334 bearings (MAE 0.169°). Its Irish SRTM variant scored 727 (MAE 0.229°). This edition extends Wales and Tenerife to 50 km. A bearing can disappear from the longer run if either bracketing ray crosses missing terrain. The archive’s previous-edition directory retains the earlier report, bearing residuals and histograms.

Of the 98 Welsh bearings complete at 15 km that lose coverage at 50 km, the first missing terrain sample lies between 15.01 and 44.10 km (median 21.51 km). Fourteen of the 28 retained bearings come from the Gors Fawr stone circle. This changes the relative weight of individual observing setups in the pooled result.

The Welsh 15 and 50 km results use the public package profiles. The intermediate Welsh check reproduces the bracketing rays at their original radial spacing. In Tenerife, all complete 50 km bearings are from Barranco de la Tapia. Correctly masking the −32768 empty cells in the Spanish terrain service left the original 15 km Tenerife and 20 km La Nariz results unchanged.

The Spanish third-coverage programme publishes finer terrain products. The archive identifies the official map sheets intersected by each measured direction. Numerical finer-resolution coverage along those rays still needs verification. The scored Tenerife results use 5 m cells.
