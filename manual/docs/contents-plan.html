1  Methods by research question

Use this guide to move from a research question to its methods chapter, principal functions and validation evidence. Open question identifies a comparison that remains to be carried out. For alphabetical lookup and individual help pages, use the function index.

1.1 Time, coordinates and ephemerides

The astronomical conventions chapter traces a BCE date across two calendars, compares lunar positions from two origins and follows the fixed-star calculation across epochs.

Existing evidence. The ephemeris chapter identifies the inspected data generation and the supplier’s precision claim.

Open question. What positional differences remain against the matching JPL solution when epoch, time scale, origin and reference frame are held fixed?

Entry points. BC.AD(), astroYear(), jd2time(), time2jd(), long.date(), timestring(), ten(), skyscapeR.vars(), obliquity(), parallax.corr(), body.position().

Ephemeris background.

1.2 Reducing field observations

The field reduction chapter follows a compass reading and a timed solar sight from instrument readings to true bearing, including a circle reading that crosses north.

Existing evidence. The horizon studies use published survey bearings. Their residuals do not isolate the reduction step.

Open question. Can an independently oriented baseline be recovered from repeat compass and solar-reference observations, including the transition across north?

Entry points. mag.dec(), reduct.compass(), reduct.theodolite(), sunAz().

1.3 Terrain horizons and panoramas

The terrain methods explain how measured profiles, DEM skylines and reconstructed features enter a horizon object. The photographic calibration and interchange chapter traces a mask through control-point fitting, angular sampling and landscape export.

Existing evidence. The international H01 study reports field agreement for DEM-derived skyline altitudes by observing setup and terrain product, including the effect of search distance. H02 compares independently computed skylines at matched viewpoints.

Open question. How well are narrow features recovered between sampled bearings? How closely does a calibrated photographic skyline match surveyed points from the same viewpoint?

Entry points. createHor(), horFeature(), hor2alt(), importPanorama(), importPeakFinder(), createHWT(), downloadHWT(), exportHor(), exportStellarium(), exportPanorama(), demPalettes(), relinkHor(), plot.skyscapeR.horizon().

1.4 From bearings to celestial targets

The candidate-screening chapter takes a measured bearing and skyline altitude through the declination calculation to candidate targets. It explains how the spatial equinox convention and the search dates affect the candidates returned.

Existing evidence. The horizon comparisons constrain one input. The worked stellar study checks a different output, a seasonal date.

Open question. Where do high or asymmetric skylines change candidate membership, and how closely do the declination limits agree with independently calculated sightlines?

Entry points. az2dec(), hor2max.dec(), hor2min.dec(), findTargets(), skyObject(), sky.objects(), zenith(), antizenith(), orbit(), spatial.equinox().

1.5 Solar, lunar and planetary events

The event-search chapter distinguishes the first and last crossing of an irregular skyline. The lunar crossover chapter explains how the observing calendar and event definition determine which lunation is selected.

Existing evidence. The EFM and CC help pages record comparisons with published simulations and remaining discrepancies. The ephemeris chapter identifies the upstream assumptions.

Open question. Which discrepancies survive matching observing geometry, event definition and calendar? Can a reference calculation recover brief or repeated crossings of an uneven skyline?

Entry points. riseset(), dS(), jS(), eq(), nMjLX(), nmnLX(), sMjLX(), smnLX(), solar.date(), moonphase(), planet.extremes(), EFM(), CC().

1.6 Stellar positions and seasonal visibility

The stellar-phases chapter follows a catalogue position through the epoch calculation, extinction and visibility tests. It distinguishes the phase on the schematic seasonal axis from the Julian date used for a sky reconstruction.

Existing evidence. The worked heliacal-rising chapter compares two visibility models over seven stars with comparable dates. The models share ephemeris and site inputs.

Open question. How well do first and last visibility dates agree with dated sightings under documented observing conditions? Which phase transitions are still untested?

Entry points. star(), star.phases(), plot.skyscapeR.starphases().

1.7 Sky brightness and visualisation

The sky reconstruction chapter follows the direct, diffuse and atmospheric light components from skyRadiance() to the display. Its Milky Way comparison shows which structures the adopted boundary and brightness classes retain.

Existing evidence. The register distinguishes outline derivation, held-out twilight measurements, conservation of flux across zoom levels and an extinction implementation check.

Open question. Which independent observations constrain the outer Milky Way boundary and low-altitude surface brightness? How does performance change outside the twilight geometries already tested?

Entry points. sketchSky(), skyRadiance(), planisphere(), importConstellations(), stellariumSky(), skyStep().

Sky-rendering evidence.

1.8 Uncertainty and orientation distributions

The orientation-density chapter shows how a measured opening becomes a bearing distribution, how the local skyline changes its declination distribution, and what is lost when disjoint highest-density intervals are reduced to a single range.

Existing evidence. The doorway example records the transformed densities for the same measured opening against a level skyline and a notch.

Open question. Does the transformation preserve probability mass at north and near declination limits? Can multimodal examples recover the expected disjoint intervals?

Entry points. az.pdf(), dec.pdf(), coordtrans(), spd(), hpdi(), subset.skyscapeR.pdf(), plot.skyscapeR.pdf(), plot.skyscapeR.spd(), plotAzimuth(), plotBars().

1.9 Null models and significance

The significance chapter documents the scientific hypothesis represented by each null, what counts as a trial, the handling of repeated sites, simulation size, and how global and local results should be read.

Existing evidence. Existing package checks include stored significance outputs. Those checks address numerical reproducibility; calibration of false-positive rates is a separate question.

Open question. Under data generated by each specified null, how often does the test reject at its stated threshold? How does that rate change with sample size and repeated measurements?

Entry points. randomTest(), modelTest(), bernoulli.trial(), pval2stars(), plot.skyscapeR.sigTest(), print.skyscapeR.sigTest().
