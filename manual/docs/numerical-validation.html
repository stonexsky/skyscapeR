19  Calendar, density and significance checks

A01, D01 and S03 tested the unmodified skyscapeR 2.0.9005 source archive, SHA-256 0446c0a57afe16274e7ef033113ac8d9f251b4f812b1a2d2ea22eac145bb3bbe. Download the exact tested package. D01 and S03 were subsequently rerun on build 2.0.9006, SHA-256 0a97b65385bb253b75b287f160b6e79da63a0ddae29b6be31c5ad6902c6cd6d2. Each study uses a numerical reference independent of the implementation under test. Each archive contains the scripts, expected and observed quantities and reproduction instructions. The previous chapters identify the builds used for their own examples.

19.1 A01 · Calendar, clock and angular conventions

Question. Can a civil date or historical era label be placed on the correct UT Julian date and converted back with the intended clock, calendar and sign?

Outcome. All 499 assertions passed. The test compared 240 absolute Julian dates and independently checked 240 returned civil strings. The maximum difference in the Julian-date comparisons was 0.000021 seconds, below the 0.0001-second tolerance. The remaining 19 assertions covered era parsing and formatting, angular signs, clock changes, invalid inputs, midnight rounding and session settings.

We computed the Julian day number by integer Gregorian or Julian calendar arithmetic, with the day starting at noon, then applied the stated clock offset. Reference anchors included J2000 noon (JD 2451545.0), the Unix epoch at midnight (JD 2440587.5) and the Julian epoch at noon (JD 0). Ten dates from astronomical year −4712 to CE 2024, four times of day, both proleptic calendars and three fixed offsets (−03:30, UTC and +05:45) formed the crossed grid. The inverse checks supplied reference JDs directly to the package. The JPL J2000 definition supplies an external anchor.

The London checks included the missing and repeated 01:30 local times on the 2024 clock-change dates; both produced errors, as documented. Year zero, Julian leap-year behaviour, a negative sub-degree angle, a date rounded across midnight, argument overrides and restoration of session timezone were also exercised. These checks cover civil-time conversion; they provide no constraint on prehistoric ΔT or the accuracy of every historical timezone database entry.

19.2 D01 · Measurement densities, sums and highest-density regions

Question. Do sampled measurement densities retain the expected shape, mass and circular support, and do summaries and subsets retain the intended observation?

Current outcome · 2.0.9006. All 173 original assertions passed, including the 18 previous discrepancies. Thirteen further checks verified broad wrapped Gaussians, the closing segment of interpolated azimuth sums and equivalent input bearings. A 0.005° grid now retains 359.995° at its own bearing. Gaussian contributions and all copies of a uniform support interval are combined at each bearing before applying the density cutoff. Declination subsets now carry centres in the selected order, including repeated and empty selections. Download the corrected D01 run.

Previous run · 2.0.9005. Of 173 assertions, 155 passed and 18 showed differences. Of 144 ordinary-width sampled-density comparisons, 132 agreed. All 12 differences occurred at the north seam on the 0.005° grid. The two additional broad-width cases and four declination-centre metadata checks also differed. The archive retains all residuals, including failures.

For each coordinate we evaluated Gaussian and uniform densities directly from their analytic definitions. Azimuth distances were taken around the circle, while declination was confined to [−90°, 90°]. The matrix comprised four azimuth centres and five declination centres, uncertainty 2°, four grid spacings and two density cutoffs. We compared every retained value, total grid mass and the first two moments about the centre. An independent numerical integral checked the retained Gaussian mass at three spacings. The summed and unit-area normalised SPDs agreed in the tested cases. Known highest-density regions, including disconnected supports and ties at threshold, returned the expected endpoints and reached the requested mass. For equal-height modes, retaining the full tied set can exceed the target mass. These are discrete-grid checks of the PDFs in the framework of Silva (2020).

Finding on build 2.0.9005 Consequence
On the 0.005° azimuth grid, the implementation folds samples above 359.99° across north. Twelve narrow-density cases centred at 359°, 0° or 1° differed from the periodic reference. Density can be displaced across the seam. For the 0° Gaussian, the maximum pointwise discrepancy was 0.19947 per degree even though the integrated mass was nearly unchanged.
A 100° uniform half-width and a Gaussian standard deviation of 100° both exceeded the effective 90° margin used for folding. The resulting curves omit part of the periodic density. A grid-spacing change alone cannot recover those tails.
Selecting the second declination PDF, reversing the order, repeating a selection or selecting no rows retained the original declination-centre vector. The selected curves and names followed the requested order. Reading metadata$dec as the centres of the selected curves can misidentify an observation.

19.3 S03 · Monte Carlo ranks, local clusters and reported probabilities

Question. Given the same observed and simulated curves, do the two significance tests report the expected global rank, local clusters and adjusted probabilities?

Current outcome · 2.0.9006. The independent scorer agreed on global ranks, local endpoints and adjusted probabilities in all 19 comparisons. These include the two original public simulation matrices replayed unchanged. All 88 binomial, print, symbol and plot assertions passed. The count fell from 92 because the two example fits now each report one cluster across north. Twelve additional checks confirmed invariance of the ranks to rotation of the angular origin and agreement between memory and disk scoring. Download the corrected S03 run.

On the original randomTest() curves, the corrected global probability is 0.4375 and the 349.5°–10° excess has adjusted p = 0.1875. On the original modelTest() curves the corrected excess spans 346°–15°, with global and adjusted local p = 0.0625. A start bearing greater than the end bearing denotes a single interval running clockwise through north. The uniform-bearing null samples north once; the global score counts each direction once, and the observed curve and every simulated curve undergo the same circular cluster search. The repeated endpoint may still appear in the display. Declination ranges remain linear. Stored fits from previous builds retain their original probabilities; refit the original input data to obtain current inference.

Previous run · 2.0.9005. Independent enumeration reproduced every global probability, local interval and adjusted local probability in 17 linear-grid cases. These included fixed fixtures and seeded calls through both public functions. All 92 separate assertions on binomial probabilities, symbol thresholds, printed results and plotted cluster marks passed. A circular reference exposed a seam effect on the local results and, in one seeded fit, on the global p-value.

The Python reference pooled the observed and simulated values at each coordinate, computed sample standard deviations and interpolated quantiles, and ranked the sum of standardised excursions. One-tail tests used upper excursions and two-tail tests also included lower excursions. For each simulated curve the reference selected its own largest qualifying cluster before ranking the observed local score. The discrete reference included ties, all-identical curves, an isolated excursion, clusters on both sides, and spans at and just below 0.5°. For public calls, the R harness recorded the actual simulation matrix passed to inference; Python then scored it independently. This isolates the scoring stage from the null-generation and power studies scheduled under S01 and S02.

What changed since the published algorithms? Silva (2020) used simulation-only bounds and scored simulations over the observed local interval. Silva & Lomsdalen (2025) extended testing to shared and site-specific null models. The present functions pool the observation with simulations, include tied exceedances, use the positive rank floor and search for the largest cluster in every simulation (Silva 2020; Silva and Lomsdalen 2025). On a fixed set of 16 curves, a reconstruction of the archived 2020 scoring equations gave global p = 0 and local p = 0 for the excess over 2–9°. Build 2.0.9005 gave 0.125 for both with the same curves. In a separate eight-simulation example with zero exceedances, the positive floor was 1/9. This comparison concerns scoring on controlled inputs. The original archaeological datasets were not reanalysed. Historical implementation and methodological comparison.

North seam on 2.0.9005. The earlier build searches the azimuth grid as a line, splitting a north-crossing excess into separate intervals. A circular diagnostic joined the first and last runs and counted the duplicated 0°/360° direction once. In the seeded 15-simulation randomTest() example, the package gave global p = 0.375 and separate intervals 349.5–360° (adjusted p = 0.750) and 0–10° (p = 0.9375). The circular comparison gave global p = 0.4375 and one 349.5–10° interval (p = 0.1875). The small number of simulations makes the arithmetic transparent. These values illustrate sensitivity to the seam and supply no estimate of false-positive frequency. Declination has physical endpoints and uses the linear comparison.

19.5 D02 · Bearing uncertainty on flat and irregular skylines

We tested coordtrans() at 50° N with a constant 2° skyline and refraction disabled. Bearings had a Gaussian standard deviation of 2° about centres of 90°, 359° and 0°. Independent Gaussian altitude errors had a standard deviation of 0.5°. For each centre, we transformed one million paired draws through the spherical altitude equation and compared the median and both endpoints of the equal-tailed 95% interval with the package density. We used the same seeded draws at each centre.

At calculation and azimuth integration spacings of 0.1° and 0.05°, all 18 quantile comparisons fell within the specified 0.02° tolerance. The largest absolute discrepancy was 0.00505°, at the upper endpoint for a bearing centred on north on the coarser grid. All six returned densities integrated to one within 0.000001. The discrepancy includes Monte Carlo sampling variation. The extended study varied each numerical spacing separately. Ten configurations returned densities on five grids, and an eleventh tested the documented zero-width limit. The references were (1) direct spherical propagation of 524,288 scrambled Sobol draws and (2) independent quadrature over 8,192 bearing strata using the package’s endpoint-Gaussian implementation of the conditional-density method (Silva 2020). The refracted case shared the Swiss altitude correction to isolate propagation. The archive reports mass error, maximum CDF distance, quantiles, prominent modes and 80% highest-density regions.

Across nine configurations away from the celestial-pole limit, all three quantiles on all five grids agreed with direct propagation within 0.008°. The maximum CDF distance was 0.00411. For the summed-density check, transforming a normalised sum of two bearing PDFs gave area 0.99999947. Grid sensitivity. The ridge had three prominent modes near −0.77°, 3.87° and 9.89°. On the default grid, the 80% highest-density region split the middle band and returned four intervals. Reducing the calculation and azimuth integration steps to 0.05° and the output step to 0.005° recovered the three reference intervals. For this ridge, refining only the integration spacing reduced the largest quantile discrepancy from 0.00645° to 0.00245°. Celestial-pole limit. At latitude 50° N with a 47° northern skyline, the fine-grid result differed from direct propagation by up to 0.1715° in quantiles and 0.0589 in cumulative probability. At a 50° northern skyline, the transformed endpoints coincide for the central bearing. The function raised its documented zero-width error on every grid even though direct propagation had a central 95% interval of 87.071°–89.819°. These cases distinguish the celestial-pole geometry from a geographic-pole observer, for which a density was recovered.

19.6 S01 · Calibration of the uniform-orientation test

How frequently does randomTest() reject when orientations have been independently drawn from its stated uniform-bearing model? How frequently does it detect a cluster of known strength and width? We fixed the survey geometry, measurement widths, sample size and bearing support before drawing each dataset.

Implementation check. In 2.0.9006 the simulated PDFs used the constructor’s default spacing and cutoff. The shared-horizon simulation also summed the bearings at the default SPD spacing, whereas the observed sum used the requested spacing. With identical centres of 30° and 120°, widths of 22° and 32°, a 30° source grid, a 0.0001 source cutoff and a 2° test spacing, maximum absolute observed–simulated density differences were 0.00203 for azimuth, 0.02020 for the shared skyline and 0.17537 for site-paired declinations. On 2.0.9007 all three differences were zero. The correction can affect probabilities calculated with non-default source settings and the shared-horizon route whenever its selected SPD spacing differed from the simulation default. Refit affected analyses from their original inputs. The 2.0.9007 socket worker setup also resolves the parent library path on each worker. In a two-library installation, the corrected serial and two-worker results agreed exactly on all three routes.

Design. We ran 2,000 independent outer datasets in each of 13 null settings and four cluster alternatives. Every outer dataset had fresh Monte Carlo reference draws. Normal and uniform measurement families, sample sizes of 2 to 24, one and two tails, different grid widths and 9, 19, 39 or 99 simulations were represented. For each outer fit we recorded the global probability and the smallest maximum-cluster-adjusted probability across the reported intervals, assigning one when there was no interval. The preselected thresholds were 1%, 5% and 10%. Finite ranks have spacing 1/(B+1)1/(B+1), with inclusive ranking for ties.

To make repeated calculations feasible we reused deterministic package-generated densities for each permitted bearing, width and site. For the shared route we tabulated the entire transform for every ordered input pair. A separate implementation scored the pooled observed and simulated curves. In 16 public-interface replays, all reconstructed curves agreed within 8×10−158\times10^{-15} in density and all global and minimum local probabilities agreed exactly. The shared-route design uses two observations and six or twelve possible directions. Its calibration applies to those designs.

Null results. Entries give the rejection percentage at the nominal 5% level, with pointwise 95% Wilson intervals over datasets. A local rejection means at least one adjusted interval crossed the threshold.

Power. With twelve measurements and 39 simulations, injecting three bearings from a 24° standard-deviation cluster centred at north gave 9.30% global rejection. These estimates describe the specified support, widths, horizons and alternatives. Each null result assumes independent orientations with the stated measurement model.

The study used five unequally spaced candidate directions, weights interpreted as probability masses, eight observations and 39 fresh reference draws per fit. We tested azimuth densities, directly constructed declination densities and asymmetric templates shifted to candidate means.

With a 0.5° source grid and 0.002 cutoff the largest observed–simulated density discrepancy was 0.0015831 in both fixtures. The same draws on 2.0.9008 gave zero discrepancy. Public fits agreed with independent assembly of their observed and simulated curves and with independently scored global and local ranks. An independent first-moment calculation also checked asymmetric template shifting, explicit replacement uncertainties and the pole-boundary error. Two actual horizon-transformed PDFs agreed with independent shifts to within floating-point precision.

19.8 H07 · Coverage of partial photographic skylines

A photograph covering 330° through north to 30° supplies no terrain height at 180°. In the archived manual example, 2.0.9007 returned a finite declination distribution spanning −39.302° to −35.032° for a uniform bearing of 180° ± 1°, and a sunrise at 92.481° on 20 March 2026. Direct altitude lookup already returned a missing value at 180°. The shifted profile rows intended to support interpolation at north also allowed other consumers to interpolate across the missing sector.

Build 2.0.9008 checks coverage at the point of use. In the archived example both the unsupported transformation and the sunrise search now give explicit coverage errors. The complete test suite also supplied an independent level mask with known edges.

Operation Verified behaviour
hor2alt() Both edges and bearings inside the photographed arc return finite values. Outside bearings return missing values.
coordtrans() Inside and north-wrapping densities agree with the complete level skyline. Positive density crossing an edge, or lying wholly outside, raises an error. Zero SPD tails outside coverage were accepted in the tested case.

exportHor() Partial photographs are rejected for both textured and numerical landscape export. Both modes accept the full photograph.

19.11 A08 · Target screening and dated bands

A measured band can intersect a target briefly, more than once, or exactly at a turning point. We compared findTargets() with independent annual stellar samples and a separate line-segment intersection calculation. For solar and lunar limits, the reference evaluates January obliquity and the local parallax term for every integer year. All 129 assertions passed on 2.0.9009.

Stellar membership. In a 30–42 CE search, a narrow band around a turning point for Sirius returned 35–35 CE and 38–39 CE after rounding the interpolated boundaries to years. The exact annual maximum gave one contact in 36 CE. The study also tested magnitude inclusion and exclusion, one-year searches, catalogue identifiers and aliases, and the pairing of colours and styles with their targets. The implementation treats stellar differences within 0.0000001° of an edge as a contact. The reference shares the stellar ephemeris. This study validates selection and aggregation; stellar positions at subannual extrema remain outside this model.

Solar and lunar bounds. Earlier builds could collapse a short overlay range to one sampled value and lose the observing site’s parallax when calculating a dated envelope.
