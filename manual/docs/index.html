skyscapeR technical manual

Methods and validation · draft of 8 November 2026

About this edition

This technical manual explains how skyscapeR turns field observations into astronomical and statistical results, which choices affect their interpretation, and what evidence supports them. The 8 November 2026 draft is organised around skyscapeR 2.0.9004. Each validation study identifies the build on which its results were obtained.

If you are new to skyscapeR, start with the worked vignette, which follows field measurements through a complete analysis. The package README gives installation instructions and guidance on migrating to version 2.0.

Finding a method

If you know the function name, start with the function index. It covers all 69 exported functions and seven registered S3 methods. If you are starting from an archaeological question, use the annotated contents. Its nine proposed chapters follow the work from time conventions and field reduction through terrain modelling, celestial events, sky visualisation, orientation distributions and significance testing. Each chapter outline identifies the evidence already available and the questions still open.

Worked chapters available in this draft

All nine areas in the annotated contents now have extended methodological treatment. The final three chapters cover astronomical conventions and stellar positions, compass and theodolite reduction, and photographic calibration and horizon interchange. Other chapters cover lunar crossover selection and comparison with the 2012 simulations and celestial candidates and limiting declinations. Earlier worked chapters follow a doorway measurement through its declination distribution and a rising body through successive terrain crossings. Other worked chapters cover terrain horizons and reconstructed features, stellar visibility, random and specified orientation models, and sky reconstruction and visual radiance. The function methods catalogue provides version-matched technical entries across all nine subject areas, with direct links to individual help pages. The annotated contents identify outstanding questions for independent validation.

The vignette guide follows all four parts of the package workflow into the methods, including dispatch through plot(). Each catalogue entry now pairs the implementation’s formal arguments with its documented behaviours and return value.

Finding the evidence

The H01 international field comparison measures how accurately createHor() recovers skyline altitude from a DEM at surveyed bearings. The study register also covers Milky Way shape, twilight, flux and extinction in sketchSky(). The heliacal-rising comparison assesses event timing. The swephR and Swiss Ephemeris chapter describes the supplier’s precision claims and the independent positional check still outstanding.
