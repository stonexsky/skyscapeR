4  swephR and the Swiss Ephemeris

Scope. This page records the astronomical dependency and its manufacturer’s accuracy claim. The documented visibility and terrain studies address different quantities from the planetary and lunar position errors discussed here.

4.1 Which layer supplies the result?

Layer Role in an analysis
skyscapeR Chooses the body, date, site and calculation settings. In 2.0.9004 its file-backed planetary position routine checks for paired planetary and lunar file coverage at the epoch, requests Swiss files and checks the returned ephemeris flag and messages. A missing file can therefore stop a calculation.
swephR Provides R bindings to the Swiss Ephemeris routines. The R package and the underlying engine have separate versions. The wrapper documentation describes the file-free Moshier option and the optional swephRdata distribution (swephR maintainers, n.d.).
Swiss Ephemeris and its data files The engine reads the available numerical ephemeris and applies the requested transformations. The compressed planetary and lunar files identify their parent JPL solution. File generation and temporal coverage must be recorded for the position being compared (Astrodienst, n.d.b, n.d.a).

The environment inspected for the 7 November pilot had swephR 0.3.2, Swiss Ephemeris 2.10.03 and swephRdata 0.0.1. The headers of the planetary and lunar files inspected for the modern and prehistoric blocks (sepl_18.se1, semo_18.se1, seplm36.se1, semom36.se1) identify DE431. Astrodienst reports a transition to DE441 in May 2026. An installation can continue to use older files. The four headers establish the provenance of these files only. They do not establish the data used for a different run (Astrodienst, n.d.b).

The event-search examples were subsequently run with modern planetary and lunar files whose headers identify DE441. The accompanying example archive records both hashes. These runs illustrate event selection and do not test compression accuracy.

4.2 What the quoted accuracy measures

Astrodienst reports 0.001 arcsecond precision for its compressed ephemeris in reproducing the parent JPL data (Astrodienst, n.d.b). This specifies agreement between two numerical representations of the same underlying lunar and planetary trajectories. A comparison against another release also includes differences between the two JPL solutions. JPL makes a corresponding distinction between the precision of its numerical representation and the uncertainty in the trajectory itself (Jet Propulsion Laboratory, n.d.).

For an archaeological horizon event, another question is where the body would have appeared to a person at a particular place and time. The answer also depends on the observer’s position, the skyline, atmospheric refraction and Earth rotation. For ancient epochs, the conversion between dynamical time and Earth-rotation time involves an uncertain ΔT (Espenak, n.d.). A fixed star brings in its catalogue position and motion as well. The quoted planetary and lunar figure therefore supplies no general error bound for stellar phase dates or horizon bearings.
