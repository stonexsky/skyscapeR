skyscapeR 2.0.9004

Inspect the linear light field of a natural sky

Description

Samples a natural sketch before display compression or masking by local terrain. One integrated flux unit is a magnitude-zero V-band source.

Usage

skyRadiance(sky, xrange = c(0, 360), yrange = c(0, 90), size = c(720, 180))

Arguments

sky

A natural sketch from sketchSky.

xrange

Increasing true azimuth limits in degrees. A sector may cross north, for example c(340,380).

yrange

Increasing altitude limits within -90 to 90 degrees.

size

Positive integer column and row counts, up to 1600 by 1000.

Details

Direct light includes stars, residual cluster light, planetary discs and the illuminated lunar disc. The Milky Way uses the accepted tracing and a provisional Carina surface-brightness calibration. Stars remain unresolved as the view is enlarged. See sketchSky for sources, assumptions and interpretation.

Value

A list containing azimuth and altitude pixel centres, total radiance, direct light (stars, residual cluster light, planets and lunar disc), and Milky Way radiance. Matrices are in magnitude-zero flux units per square degree, ordered from high to low altitude and increasing azimuth. Multiply by area (square degrees per pixel by row) and sum to obtain flux. The difference between total and the two source matrices is the atmospheric background. The Sun contributes to the background; its disc is a plot overlay.
